**Findings:**

  the raw Trial number to the recurrent layers, and told Opher so; supplying rescaled
  Trial numbers improved the loss only a little, so that is not the explanation, or not
  all of it. Cause unknown; set aside at Opher's request.

- Mermaid diagrams render in the GitHub wiki. Without Node on this computer they can be
  previewed through mermaid.ink, which is sent the diagram's text.
- `pkill -f <pattern>` inside a compound shell command also kills that command.

**Decisions:**
- Opher: use `TimeSeriesNetwork` with the padding flag; if the full run converges and

- The choice of summary network is worth a decision record:
  [0007](../decisions/0007-summary-network-for-sittings.md), proposed, in the C3b pull

## Session 1, continued — no padding: one Schedule per batch

**Done:**
- The padded full run was stopped at Opher's word (about 20 minutes in; its data and
  outputs deleted) and step C3b was rebuilt without padding
  ([1841a5c](https://github.com/opherdonchin/MotorAdpatationSBI/commit/1841a5c)):
  `simulate_experiment` gained `n_sittings_per_schedule`; the notebook trains online, each
  batch 32 Sittings on one Schedule; the test Experiment is 30 Schedules with 10 Sittings
  each. Tiny end-to-end trial passed; the full run (100 epochs of 300 batches, 960,000
  Sittings) was started.
- [Decision 0007](../decisions/0007-summary-network-for-sittings.md) rewritten for this;
  wiki page Concept-summary-networks updated.

**Findings:**
- A trained BayesFlow summary network takes any length; only Sittings within a batch must
  be equally long. I had padded because we had agreed to train from a saved Experiment
  with one Schedule per Sitting, and did not put the alternative to Opher before starting
  a 3-hour run. He raised it himself.
- JAX compiles the training step again for each new Sitting length: about 4.5 s per new
  length, against about 0.12 s for a step at a known length (one-off probe, 60 batches).
  Hence training Schedules are cut to a multiple of 25 Trials.
- Simulating a batch of 32 Sittings takes about 0.08 s.

**Decisions:**
- Opher: batches have a fixed length, by reorganizing how the Simulator is run; no
  padding.
- Mine, for review: online training (fresh Sittings every batch) instead of a saved
  training Experiment, against BayesFlow's "offline first" guidance, because BayesFlow's
  offline dataset needs one array; 960,000 Sittings in all; the cut to multiples of 25
  Trials; a validation batch on a single Schedule.

## Session 1, continued — results of the full run

**Done:**
- The full run of
  [one_state_posterior.ipynb](../../notebooks/working/one_state_posterior.ipynb) finished
  (1.18 hours of training, about 5 minutes of test sampling). Results written into the
  notebook (`#[results]`) and [docs/models/one_state.md](../models/one_state.md).

**Findings** (all from the executed notebook unless marked):
- Training: loss from 1.7 to about 0.31; last 10 epochs 0.307 (training) and 0.304
  (validation), the 10 before 0.312 and 0.324. Nearly flat, still creeping down.
- Recovery (correlation of posterior median with truth): A 0.93, B 0.99, sigma_eta 0.89,
  sigma_epsilon 1.00. Contraction 0.997, 0.993, 0.84, 0.994.
- Calibration passes for A, sigma_eta and sigma_epsilon (error about 0.01, rank curves
  inside the band). It fails mildly for B: error 0.05, Log Gamma -3.2, rank curve outside
  the band by up to 0.11.
- One-off calculation (scratch script, fresh posterior draws from the saved Engine, not
  in the notebook): for B the posterior mean is on average 0.15 posterior standard
  deviations below the truth, the median about 1.5% low, and the 50% interval holds the
  truth 57% of the time. For the other three the mean offset is at most 0.05 standard
  deviations.
- By Sitting length (150 Sittings each half): recovery better for longer Sittings;
  calibration errors 0.02 to 0.08 in both halves, no clear difference.
- The Engine was tested on Schedule lengths off the training grid of multiples of 25.
- The padded probe earlier today had contraction 0.48 for A after 1,700 Sittings; with
  960,000 it is 0.997. The amount of training, not the architecture, was the limit there.

**Open questions / next:** whether the miscalibration of B is under-training (the loss
was still falling) or something lasting: more epochs would tell. Pull request #24's
explanation; then, with Opher, where next.
