  log odds with the mass in $(0.75, 0.999)$ and $(0.01, 0.5)$.
- No fixed schedule: sittings of varying length, blocks of 0, +1 or -1 with lengths
  mostly between 10 and 50 trials and random transitions.

## Session 2, continued — scope #12 agreed; PR 1 started

**Done:**
- Opher agreed scope #12 (recorded as a comment on the issue). He expects identification
  trouble with short sittings; check 4 is reported by sitting length for that reason. The
  likelihood is for a whole sitting, not a trial.
- Branch `sim/one-state-kalman`, draft PR #13.
- `notebooks/explore_one_state.ipynb`: prior and schedule samplers, simulator, Kalman
  log-likelihood, brute-force joint Gaussian, and exact-posterior SBC (checks 1 to 4).
- `analyses/sim/one_state/README.md`; `.gitignore` now ignores everything under
  `analyses/` except READMEs. Model math in `docs/models/one_state.md`.

**Findings:**
- `pymc-extras` (PyMC's state-space models) requires PyMC < 6.3, so it cannot be used
  with the pinned 6.3; the Kalman filter is a hand-written `pytensor.scan`.
- The filter is written once, in PyTensor, and compiled with `pytensor.function` for the
  numerical checks, so there is one definition and not a NumPy copy.
- In PyMC 6.3 `pm.LogitNormal` gets no automatic transform (sampling fails at the start
  point); the priors are `pm.Normal` on the unbounded scale instead, which is also how
  decision 0002 states them.
- A `pm.Potential` is not stored with the draws. The sitting is therefore an observed
  `pm.CustomDist` whose `logp` is the Kalman filter, and `pm.compute_log_likelihood`
  gives the per-draw log-likelihood used as an SBC test quantity.
- One exact fit (4 chains, 1000 + 1000 draws, `cores=1`) takes 4 to 10 seconds.

**Decisions:**
- The prior is written twice, as a NumPy sampler and as the PyMC model, both reading the
  constants in `#[config]`. SBC is the check that they agree. To raise in the PR.
