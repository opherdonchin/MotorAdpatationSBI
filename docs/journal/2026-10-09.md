**Findings:**
- Downsampling is optional in BayesFlow, off by default. When on, it is a learned
  convolution of width 3 applied to the raw inputs with a stride equal to the factor, so
  with a factor above 3 some Trials are never read at all.
- `TimeSeriesTransformer`: every Trial attends to every other; memory grows with batch
  size times the square of the Sitting length. `FusionTransformer`: a recurrent pass
  plus attention; same growth. `TimeSeriesNetwork`: convolutions at full resolution, then
  a recurrent network that reads every Trial (plus a second branch reading every fourth);
  memory grows only in proportion to length, but it takes no padding mask.
- Probe, base-size networks, flow matching, demo Experiment padded to 2,266 Trials, time
  per training step in the second epoch:

  | Summary network | Trials kept | Batch | ms per step |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | TimeSeriesTransformer | 500 | 32 | 28 |
  | TimeSeriesTransformer | 1,000 | 32 | 100 |
  | TimeSeriesTransformer | 1,000 | 8 | 25 |
  | TimeSeriesTransformer | 2,266 | 4 | 58 |
  | FusionTransformer | 1,000 | 32 | 136 |
  | FusionTransformer | 2,266 | 8 | 217 |
  | TimeSeriesNetwork (no mask) | 2,266 | 32 | 169 |

  So nothing needs downsampling: full-length Sittings fit with a small batch
  (transformers) or with the recurrent network. For 20,000 Sittings and 100 epochs that
  is roughly 8 hours (transformer, batch 4) or 3 hours (recurrent network, batch 32) on
  this computer. Most of that is padding: Sittings have a median of about 700 Trials but
  every one is padded to the longest, 2,266.
